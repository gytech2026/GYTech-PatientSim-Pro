## 1. Not a medical device

GYTech PatientSim Pro has not been cleared, approved, certified, or registered as
a medical device by any regulatory authority. It is not designed or intended to
support diagnosis, treatment, monitoring, resuscitation, or clinical decision
making for a real patient.

## 2. No actual patient monitoring

The application does not connect to real monitors, sensors, defibrillators,
pacemakers, hospital networks, or electronic health records. The "patient" is a
fictional software model. All vital signs, waveforms, alarms, rhythms, and device
states are simulated.

## 3. No actual electrical output

The defibrillator and pacing modules are software simulations only. No electrical
energy is generated, stored, discharged, or transmitted. No physical device is
controlled. The "shock," "charge," "energy," and "pacing" actions exist only
inside the browser or desktop application.

The desktop About dialog repeats this limitation. Installing or launching the
desktop package does not add any physical monitoring or therapy capability.

## 4. Not clinical guidance

- Waveforms are approximate educational models, not diagnostic ECGs.
- Lead morphology and 12-lead patterns are not clinically validated.
- Blood pressure, capnography, temperature, and perfusion models are simplified.
- Shockable/non-shockable classification is a teaching model based on the
  configured simulated state.
- Energy values and mode guidance are training-only settings, not universal
  clinical prescriptions.
- CPR quality is calculated from selected rate/depth and is not measured from a
  physical sensor.
- ROSC is a configured simulation event and is not a guarantee or prediction of
  a real clinical outcome.
- The AED and synchronized cardioversion workflows are simplified simulation
  workflows.

## 5. No substitute for training

This software is not a substitute for BLS, ACLS, defibrillation, airway,
pharmacology, nursing, or other accredited clinical education. It does not
qualify a person to operate real medical equipment. Users must seek appropriate
supervision, certification, and clinical training.
