## 1. Scope

This Code applies to project repositories, issue trackers, pull requests,
discussions, documentation, review comments, community chat spaces, and
representations made on behalf of the project. It applies to maintainers,
contributors, reviewers, and participants.

## 2. Expected behavior

- Communicate respectfully and precisely.
- Critique code, evidence, and behavior rather than people.
- Welcome good-faith questions from people with different levels of experience.
- Give credit where it is due and respect attribution and licensing.
- Protect privacy: never post real patient information, credentials, or private
  communications.
- Keep medical-simulation discussions within the project's educational scope.
- Disclose conflicts of interest when they could affect a technical decision.
- Follow the project's safety, licensing, and review requirements.

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, threats, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory comments or conduct based on identity, background, disability,
  religion, nationality, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, or other
  protected characteristics.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of clinical validity, regulatory status, test
  results, licensing, authorship, or project endorsement.
- Publishing real patient data, credentials, private information, or exploit
  details without responsible coordination.
- Encouraging unsafe use of the simulator as a clinical device or as a substitute
  for trained clinical care.
- Trolling, personal attacks, doxxing, retaliation, or bad-faith editing.
- Plagiarism or adding third-party assets without correct licensing and
  attribution.

## 4. Medical-simulation responsibility

Participants must:

- Treat the project as educational software only.
- Avoid guidance that would present the simulator as a medical device or
  clinically validated system.
- Flag safety concerns about pulse state, rhythm classification, defibrillation,
  cardioversion, pacing, CPR, alarms, recovery, or data handling.
- Avoid real patient data in tests, screenshots, issues, or pull requests.
- Respect the requirement that no simulated control communicates with real
  medical equipment.

## 5. Reporting

If a participant experiences or witnesses a violation, report it privately to
the project owner or the GYTech community-conduct contact when one is published.

**Configurable contact placeholder:** `[GYTECH_CODE_OF_CONDUCT_CONTACT]`

Do not invent an email address. The owner should replace that placeholder with
an official contact before public community launch. If no contact is available,
use a private channel provided by the repository host and request maintainer
attention without posting sensitive details publicly.

Reports should include, when safe:

- What happened.
- Where and when it happened.
- Who was involved.
- Whether immediate safety or privacy concerns exist.
- Any supporting evidence that can be shared privately.

## 6. Confidentiality and good faith

The project will handle conduct reports with discretion to the extent reasonably
possible. Participants making good-faith reports will not be retaliated against.
Knowingly false or malicious reports are themselves unacceptable.

## 7. Enforcement responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

- Clarifying and applying this Code consistently.
- Taking reasonable steps to protect affected participants.
- Avoiding conflicts of interest in enforcement.
- Documenting decisions and consequences privately where appropriate.
- Correcting or removing contributions that violate this Code or project
  policies.

Because the project has no confirmed legal entity or formal community team at
this time, the owner must appoint a responsible maintainer or external process
before public community adoption.

## 8. Consequences

Responses may include:

1. A private clarification or warning.
2. A request to correct or remove content.
3. Temporary restriction from participation.
4. Permanent removal from project spaces.
5. Referral to the repository host or relevant authority when required.

The response should be proportionate to impact, context, and repetition. Urgent
privacy, safety, or legal risks may require immediate restrictive action.
